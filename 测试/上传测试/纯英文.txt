The rain fell in sheets across the city, blurring the lights of passing cars into long streaks of gold and red. Marcus stood at the window of his apartment, watching the storm roll in from the east.

He had been waiting for this night for three years. Every detail had been planned, every contingency considered. But as the thunder rolled overhead, he felt something he hadn't expected: doubt.

The phone on his desk buzzed. A single message from an unknown number: "They know. Get out now."

Marcus didn't move. He had known this moment would come. The only question was whether he would be ready when it did.

The code on his screen flickered. Thirty-seven seconds until the transfer would complete. Thirty-seven seconds that felt like a lifetime. He thought about Sarah, about the last time he saw her at the airport. She had kissed him on the cheek and said "come back."

He should have told her the truth then. But some truths are too dangerous to share.

Twenty seconds now. The rain was getting heavier, hammering against the glass like a frantic heartbeat. Marcus could hear footsteps in the hallway outside. Heavy, deliberate, not trying to hide.

Fifteen seconds.

The door burst open. Marcus turned. Three men in dark suits stood in the doorway. The one in front smiled.

"Hello, Marcus. Long time no see."

"Not long enough."

Ten seconds.

"Hand over the drive. This doesn't have to get messy."

"It already is."

Five seconds.

The screen flashed green. TRANSFER COMPLETE.

Marcus smiled for the first time in three years. "You're too late. The whole world knows now."

The man's smile faded. He raised his gun. But Marcus was already moving, diving through the window into the storm.
